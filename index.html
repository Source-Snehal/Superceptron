One gets through. One doesn't.

A resume that's been generated to match your posting will beat one that wasn't, every time, on every system that scores surface similarity. That's not a bug in the matching, it's what matching does.

So you get two failure modes running simultaneously. Candidates who look like the posting and can't do the job. Candidates who can do the job and don't look like the posting.

The first wastes an interview slot. The second never gets one, and you never find out it happened, because nothing in the process is designed to tell you what it discarded.

Either way your firm's name goes on a submission because of a good sentence.

Three Models. Real Variance. One Signature.

Three independent reads across three models, grounded citation, and a person who signs the shortlist.

Single-model screening collapses a candidate into one posterior. You get a number, no distribution, and no way to interrogate it. Disagreement that existed inside the model gets averaged into confidence that was never there.

We run three reads in parallel over the same document, each on a different model. No read sees another's output. Nothing anchors, so disagreement survives to the end of the pipeline instead of being resolved before anyone can see it.

Strict

Scores against the posting's literal requirements. Absence of a stated term is treated as absence, not inferred presence. Deliberately calibrated to over-reject, because it is one vote of three and the distance between it and the transferable read is itself the signal.

Transferable

Scores capability independent of surface form. Targets the false negative: the candidate whose evidence is real and whose vocabulary doesn't match the posting's.

Adjudicative

Scores expected information gain from an interview slot. Not "is this a match" but "what would an hour tell us that the document can't." The scarce resource in hiring was never candidates. It's your hiring manager's calendar, and this is the read that allocates it.

Variance across the panel is retained as a first-class signal rather than collapsed into a mean. Tight agreement ranks directly. Real disagreement routes automatically to a person, who works the contested set and resolves it before the shortlist reaches you.

Every assertion is grounded to a span in the source document. Claims that cannot be grounded are surfaced as unsupported rather than silently discounted, because absence of evidence in a resume is not evidence of absence in a candidate.

A person adjudicates the contested set and signs the shortlist. Not because the pipeline can't emit a ranking. Because a ranking nobody will put their name to is what the rest of the market is already shipping.
