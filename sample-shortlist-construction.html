Before anything was read

What the posting asks for, and what the job needs

The posting was read first, on its own, before a single resume was opened. BIM resumes are among the hardest in construction to screen, because everyone lists the same six software packages and almost nobody says what they did with them. Modeling to design intent and modeling to fabrication are different jobs, and the resume rarely tells you which one you are getting.

This is a judgment, not a fact. It is here so you can disagree with it, and so you know what the ranking was built on.

Load-bearing

  • LOD 400, fabrication-levelThe posting says "Revit proficiency". What this job needs is someone who models spools that get built, not design intent someone else details later.
  • Has run coordination, not just clash detectionAnyone can run a Navisworks report. This job needs someone who sits in the room and negotiates with the electrical and sprinkler contractors.
  • Fabrication software, CADmep or SysQueNamed once, buried. It is the clearest single signal of whether someone has done trade-side BIM.
  • Understands what installsNot a certification. Shows up in whether the resume mentions hanger layout, access clearance, or rework.

Probably carried forward

  • "BIM Manager title required"Costs more of this pool than anything else. In trade contracting the same job is called coordinator, detailer, VDC engineer or drafter depending on who wrote the org chart.
  • "Healthcare experience essential"Med gas and above-ceiling congestion are the real challenge. Lab, semiconductor and central plant work teach the same density.
  • Autodesk Certified ProfessionalListed as required. Almost no strong trade-side coordinator holds it, and several weak candidates do.
  • Bachelor's degreeFour of the six shortlisted came through drafting programs or the trade instead.

How this was read

Three reads, then a person decides.

Every resume is read three times, from three different angles. The readers work independently. None of them sees the others' verdicts, so agreement means something and disagreement means something else.

Where all three land in the same place, the ranking is easy and you can move fast. Where they split, you get told, and you get both arguments. Those are the ones a single score would have quietly hidden.

The strict read

Scores against what the posting actually asks for. Absence of evidence counts as absence. Unforgiving on purpose.

The transferable read

Looks for evidence the person could do the work even when the sector, the vocabulary or the job title does not match.

The hiring manager

One interview slot this week. Would you spend it here, and what would you be hoping to find out?

Then the review

We read every resume ourselves alongside the three verdicts, and we write the ranking. Where the readers agree we are checking their work. Where they split we are choosing between two arguments, and we tell you which one we went with and why.

That decision is a recommendation, not a filter. Every applicant appears somewhere in this document, including the ones we ranked last.
