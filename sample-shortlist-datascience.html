Before anything was read

What the posting asks for, and what it's actually testing for

The posting was read first, on its own, before a single resume was opened. Data science postings are unusually vulnerable to vocabulary mismatch, because the same underlying skill gets described completely differently depending on where someone learned it: a PhD program, a bootcamp, or a startup with no formal training budget at all.

This is a judgment, not a fact. It is here so you can disagree with it, and so you know what the ranking was built on.

Load-bearing

  • Has shipped a model to productionThe posting says "deployment experience." What matters is whether a model actually served real traffic, not whether it lived in a notebook.
  • Genuine causal reasoning, however describedNamed as "causal inference" in the posting. The skill shows up under many names: A/B testing, natural experiments, staggered rollouts, diff-in-diff, none of which use the phrase "causal inference."
  • Can translate technical tradeoffs for non-technical stakeholdersThe posting buries this as one line. On a risk team, the ability to explain a false-positive-rate tradeoff to a VP is closer to the job than any single modeling technique.

Probably carried forward

  • "PhD required"Costs more of this pool than anything else in the posting. Several of the strongest applicants have a master's or no graduate degree and equivalent or better shipped work.
  • "Must know Databricks specifically"A reasonable preference, not a hard requirement. Anyone fluent in Spark and a comparable platform (Snowflake, BigQuery) picks this up in days.
  • "Data Scientist" as the exact job title heldSome of the best causal-reasoning work in this pile was done by people titled Analyst, because the company they worked at labels roles idiosyncratically.

How this was read

Three reads, then a person decides.

Every resume is read three times, from three different angles. The readers work independently. None of them sees the others' verdicts, so agreement means something and disagreement means something else.

Where all three land in the same place, the ranking is easy. Where they split, you get told, and you get both arguments.

The strict read

Scores against what the posting actually asks for, by name. Absence of the stated term counts as absence. Unforgiving on purpose.

The transferable read

Looks for evidence the person could do the work even when the vocabulary, the title, or the credential doesn't match.

The hiring manager

One interview slot this week. Would you spend it here, and what would you be hoping to find out?

Then the review

I read every resume myself alongside the three verdicts, and I write the ranking. Where the readers agree I'm checking their work. Where they split I'm choosing between two arguments, and I tell you which one I went with and why.

Every claim in every verdict below traces back to a specific line in the candidate's resume. Where a claim is inferred rather than stated outright, that's said plainly rather than dressed up as fact.
