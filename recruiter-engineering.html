The problem

Two forces making site &
engineering hiring harder.

Most shortlisting tools don't fix this. They make both problems worse.

Keyword tools can't tell a real certification from a typo

OSHA 10, OSHA 30, journeyman licenses, NCCER certifications, PE licenses. Every candidate spells their qualifications and past project scale slightly differently. A keyword filter treats "OSHA 30 certified" and "OSHA-30" as different things, or misses a licensed structural engineer entirely because the resume says "PE" instead of "licensed." Strong candidates disappear. Your client never meets them, and it's your reputation on the line when the placement falls through.

Site experience doesn't fit a resume template

The strongest site managers and engineers often have the least polished resumes: years of project delivery captured in a bullet list, contract types (AIA, ConsensusDocs) mentioned once in passing, scale of work buried in a project name. A trained eye reads that correctly on the first pass. An automated filter reads it as a gap, and a genuinely strong candidate never makes it to your desk.

Both problems have the same fix: someone who reads for meaning, not keywords, and knows what a strong hire on a live site actually looks like.

→ Domain expertise you can't replicate with a prompt. Our experts know engineering and construction hiring. They spot the signal that raw AI misses.
